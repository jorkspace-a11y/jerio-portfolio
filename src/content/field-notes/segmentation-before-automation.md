At [KKBC](/work/03-kkbc/), a CRM sequence delivered 1,534 emails at a 14.4% click-through rate. The commonly cited B2B benchmark range is 2 to 3%. Automation is usually the first thing credited when a number like that shows up. It wasn't the reason here.

The sequence itself, the enrollment triggers, the send schedule, the contact tagging, was automated, but that automation came after the segmentation logic and the copy were built, not instead of them. I wrote the segmentation rules and the copy for each touchpoint separately first, then automated the parts that were pure manual overhead: contact tagging, sequence enrollment, lead status updates. The automation removed roughly two hours of manual CRM work a week. It didn't produce the click-through rate. The list of who got which message, and what that message actually said, produced the click-through rate.

That's the distinction worth sitting with if you're looking at your own CRM numbers. A high send volume with weak segmentation still produces a schedule that looks automated and professional. It just doesn't convert, because everyone on the list is getting a message written for an average contact who doesn't exist. The 74 qualified leads generated from this sequence trace back to the segmentation, not the send cadence.

I want to be specific about what I can and can't claim from one engagement. The click-through rate and lead count are the actual numbers reported on this project, not projections. What I can't claim is that this exact segmentation structure will produce a similar lift on a different list, different industry, or different offer, because I haven't run the same structure elsewhere and measured it against a matched control. What transfers is the order of operations: segmentation and copy first, automation second, not the specific percentages.

If a CRM sequence is going out on schedule but the numbers aren't moving, the question worth asking isn't "should we send more" or "should we automate more." It's whether every contact on that list is actually getting a different message based on who they are, or whether everyone's getting the same sequence with automation making it look more sophisticated than it is.
